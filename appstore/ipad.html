Three stages of respiration

Cellular respiration is how cells turn glucose into usable energy, released across three stages: glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport chain.

Glycolysis takes place in the cytoplasm and splits glucose into two pyruvate molecules — it needs no oxygen and nets two ATP and two NADPH.

How do the light reactions power the Calvin cycle?
auto_fix_high
The light reactions split water and build ATP and NADPH inside the thylakoid. The Calvin cycle then spends that ATP and NADPH to fix CO₂ into G3P — so they’re the energy supply that drives carbon fixation.

Fixing carbon into sugar

The Calvin cycle runs in the stroma of the chloroplast, spending the ATP and NADPH made by the light reactions to turn CO₂ into glucose.

It is light-independent — but it can’t run for long without the light reactions constantly topping up those energy carriers.

Fig 1 · The chloroplast — light reactions in the thylakoids, the Calvin cycle in the stroma.
